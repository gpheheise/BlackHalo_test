What these numbers mean

We report every finding to the affected vendor within 24 hours of discovering it, then hold the technical details for 90 days so a fix can be built. These three figures are the honest record of that work.

Reported
Every finding our researchers have sent to a vendor — the full count, including those still under embargo. This is the headline number: the work done.
Disclosed
The subset we have since published in full, with vendor and product named — once the 90 days elapse or the vendor ships its own advisory. Lower than Reported mostly because clocks are still running.
Missed
A bug we independently found and reported, but where another researcher was publicly credited for it — the CVE or the award went to them. The same vulnerability can be discovered by more than one team; when the credit lands elsewhere we count it here rather than claim it. An honest record, not an inflated one.

AI widens the search. Specialists direct it, confirm what it finds and answer for what gets published. Every finding takes the same five steps, and the counts show where they stand today.

    Coordinated disclosure

    Questions about the record

    No vendor or product is named here before the vendor has had the chance to fix it.

    Why do most findings show no vendor or product?

    While a finding's 90-day clock runs, only its report date, class, severity and score are public. Vendor, product, title and writeup stay withheld so nobody can use them before a fix exists. A vendor is named sooner only once it has published its own advisory for the issue.

    Why is Disclosed so much lower than Reported?

    Disclosure follows the report by up to 90 days, so the gap is mostly findings whose clocks are still running.

    What happens if there is no fix after 90 days?

    The record is published anyway, so the people running the software can protect themselves. When a fix is close, the deadline can be extended in good faith, and the clock shows every extension. Each record shows whether and when a fix shipped.

    Who finds the vulnerabilities, AI or people?

    Both, in fixed roles. The agent engine widens the search and speeds up analysis. Specialists choose the targets, direct the testing, challenge what it produces and confirm every finding before it is counted. The researcher, not the tool, answers for what is reported and published.

    Is this a vulnerability scanner?

    No. Scanners repeat checks for weaknesses that are already known. Every finding here comes from research rather than a signature, and is backed by evidence that reproduces it.

    How are the scores calculated?

    Each finding carries a CVSS v3.1 vector, and its base score is computed from that vector rather than typed in. Severity follows the CVSS bands: Low 0.1–3.9, Medium 4.0–6.9, High 7.0–8.9 and Critical 9.0–10.0.

    Why BlackHalo

    Not another autonomous agent.

    Autonomous pentest agents are common now — tools like Strix, ARTEX, PentestGPT and CyberStrikeAI all spin up specialist agents, validate findings, keep state and run local models. BlackHalo isn't trying to be one more of those. What sets it apart is the discipline around the assessment: what counts as tested, what counts as proved, who is allowed to act, and an expert who stands behind the result. The record above is that discipline in practice.

    1. 01

      Coverage is a declared denominator

      An assessment names the finite set of things that must be investigated — assets, builds, identities, every render and authorization path — and tracks what is blocked, deferred or still open. "The page loaded" is not "every path was exercised." It is how missed sub-routes, wrong renderers and premature "not vulnerable" calls get caught before a report goes out.

    2. 02

      Evidence is graded, not asserted

      Every finding carries an assurance level: supported by source, reproduced in a controlled setting, or verified as a live effect in the target environment. A suspected code weakness, a lab reproduction and a proven live impact are three different claims with three different remediation priorities — and the client sees which one they have.

    3. 03

      Depth is separated from authority

      How hard BlackHalo investigates is a different setting from what it is permitted to do. Deep source analysis never silently grants more intrusive actions; in production, each action against a target needs explicit approval. Capability never turns into authority by accident.

    4. 04

      An expert owns the outcome

      The agent engine widens the search and speeds up analysis; specialists choose the targets, challenge what it produces, adjudicate every finding and write the remediation. The researcher — not the tool — answers for what is reported and published, and is the person who turns verified findings into reduced exposure.

    5. 05

      The method improves under control

      Retrospectives, versioned changes to the assessment skills, independent evaluation against unseen cases and the ability to roll back — so what BlackHalo learns from a hard run is adopted with provenance and review, not drift.

    We don't claim to find more than every other tool, or to run without people.
